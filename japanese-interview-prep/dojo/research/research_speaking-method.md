# Research: getting from N3-level speaking to interview-ready in 8 weeks (Japanese)

**Method:** about 40 generic searches in English, Japanese and Vietnamese, plus full reads of about 20 key pages and PDFs. Nothing about the candidate was searched or sent. Section 6 turns the findings into a daily protocol. Interview phrases are given in Japanese.

## 1. Fluency techniques

**Verified**
- **Shadowing steps in the interview textbook (Kurosio).** (1) Listen with the script and check meaning. (2) Shadow silently in your head. (3) Shadow while reading the script, then mumble along without it. (4) Prosody shadowing without the script, copying speed, accent, intonation, stress and pauses. (5) Content shadowing: keep that prosody while thinking about meaning. The book advises short daily sessions that repeat the same script. [source: https://www.9640.jp/BOOK_PDF/9784874249475.pdf | confidence: high | 2016]
- **Kurosio teacher workshop (迫田久美子).** It uses the same ladder and adds synchronized reading. The keys it names are daily practice, thinking about meaning, and feedback. Its comparisons found shadowing beat both reading aloud and hand-copying on more measures, and both easy and hard materials worked. I did not read the underlying studies. [source: https://9640.jp/wp-content/uploads/2020/10/2020shadowingWS_sakoda.pdf | confidence: medium | 2020]
- **Prosody-shadowing study in Japanese as a second language.** Eight Chinese-L1 learners practised for 3 months. Their production of 促音 and long vowels improved slightly, and the gains held at a delayed posttest. The sample is tiny. [source: https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/csssej/15/0/15_11/_article/-char/en | confidence: high | 2014]
- **Wood, case study of one Japanese learner of English (90 min a week for 6 weeks).**
  - Steps: a model story with its transcript (mark the pauses, notice the chunks); shadow the model at least 8 times; dictogloss and chunk-sharing; 4/3/2 (the same talk told to new partners in 4, then 3, then 2 min); record without notes; free talk.
  - Results: speech rate rose from 123 to 140 syllables/min (+13.8%). Mean length of run rose from 5.1 to 6.4 syllables (+26.3%). Pauses were counted from 0.3 s. [source: https://journals.lib.unb.ca/index.php/CJAL/article/download/19898/21737 | confidence: high | 2009]
- **de Jong and Perfetti.** With 4/3/2, fluency rose in every group during training. Only learners who repeated the same speech kept the gains at posttest. I saw the abstract only, via search. [source: https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9922.2010.00620.x | confidence: medium | 2011]
- **Suzuki and Hanzawa.** Learners told the same story six times.
  - Six times in a row cut pauses most on a new task, but slowed speech and caused more word-for-word repetition.
  - The schedule made little difference after one week.
  - So: spread repetitions within a day and across days. [source: https://doi.org/10.1017/S0272263121000358 | confidence: high | 2022]
- **Kim and Webb spacing meta-analysis (48 experiments, 3,411 learners).** Spaced practice had a medium-to-large benefit, and longer gaps did better on delayed tests. This supports spaced-repetition review of chunks. [source: https://eld.bl.uk/catalog/100152978253_0x000001 | confidence: medium | 2022]
- **Lyster and Saito feedback meta-analysis (15 classroom studies, N=827).** Prompts, where the teacher gives a hint and you correct yourself, worked better than recasts, where you just hear the corrected form. [source: https://www.cambridge.org/core/product/4999EE1C8379B2BF026B148EAF373CA1 | confidence: medium | 2010]
- **Self-transcription (Lynch 2001).** Transcribing and correcting your own recorded speech helps you notice your errors. I read this only in a secondary source. [source: https://revistas.udistrital.edu.co/index.php/calj/article/view/4989 | confidence: medium | 2015]
- **Self-imitation (Golden Speaker).** 15 learners spent 3 weeks imitating their own voice, re-synthesised with a native-like accent. Their fluency and comprehensibility improved, and the fluency gains held later. This indirectly supports shadowing your own corrected script. [source: https://iastatedigitalpress.com/psllt/article/id/15721 | confidence: medium]
- **Measuring progress.** The standard timing measures are speech rate and mean length of run (syllables between pauses). Across 22 studies, speed and the number of pauses in mid-clause best predicted how fluent listeners judged the speech. [source: https://eprints.lancs.ac.uk/id/eprint/152963 | confidence: medium | 2021]

**Reported / anecdotal**
- **瞬間作文 (adapted from 瞬間英作文).** You see a sentence in your own language and say the target sentence instantly. It uses simple patterns and many repetitions: re-drill only the failed items, then swap in your own words. It is popular in Japan, but no outcome data is given. [source: https://toraiz.jp/english-times/book/2792 | confidence: medium | 2021]

**Not found**
- Controlled studies of 瞬間作文, 4/3/2, retelling or content shadowing for Japanese-L2 interview speech.

## 2. Pronunciation and clarity

**Verified**
- **OJAD (University of Tokyo).**
  - Accent search for 9,000+ nouns and 3,500 verbs/adjectives (about 42,300 conjugated forms, with male and female audio).
  - Verb-suffix search, text search, and the prosody tutor スズキクン.
  - Has a Vietnamese interface. [source: https://www.gavo.t.u-tokyo.ac.jp/ojad/eng/pages/home | confidence: high]
- **スズキクン.** Paste a text; "/" marks a phrase break.
  - You get H/L accent marks, a pitch curve and shading on devoiced morae.
  - It reads the text aloud in voices F1, F2, M1 or M2 at speed F, N or S (e.g. "// F2 S"). There is a pitch editor.
  - Limits: each sentence is read in isolation, with no emphasis or question intonation. Names, unknown words and numbers may be misread. [source: https://www.gavo.t.u-tokyo.ac.jp/ojad/eng/pages/using/newsuzuki | confidence: high]
- **Vietnamese-L1 learners (perception test).** The most common error was hearing low-pitched short vowels as long. They also heard some short vowels as geminates. [source: https://js.vnu.edu.vn/FS/article/view/11/10 | confidence: high | 2015]
- **Online interviews.** Speak louder than usual and make nods and gestures bigger. [source: https://www.jasso.go.jp/ryugaku/after_study_j/job/__icsFiles/afieldfile/2026/10/30/guide_2028_39-77.pdf | confidence: high | 2026-10]

**Reported**
- **Loanword accent rule of thumb.** Pitch falls after the 3rd mora from the end. If that mora is ー, ッ or ン, the fall moves one mora earlier (サッカー). Many 4-mora words are flat (アルバム, コンビニ). [source: https://aiueo.cc/pages_v2/ja/pronounce/web/pw104.php | confidence: medium]
- **Speaking pace and answer length.** About 300 characters per minute. Aim for about 1 min per answer and 30 s for simple questions. Self-PR or motivation can run up to 2 min in one-to-one interviews; keep group-interview answers under 1 min. [source: https://internshipguide.jp/columns/view/interview-answer-length | confidence: medium | 2021]
- **OJAD audio cannot be reused.** Audio generated by スズキクン may not be embedded in other software or sites. [source: https://www.gavo.t.u-tokyo.ac.jp/ojad/pages/usage_suzukikun | confidence: medium]

**Not found**
- An official NHK figure for news-reading speed.

## 5. Keigo and manners

**Verified**
- **二重敬語.** This means using the same type of keigo twice on one word, e.g. お読みになられる; it is generally wrong. Accepted exceptions include お召し上がりになる, お見えになる, お伺いする and お伺いいたす. Two keigo verbs joined with て, such as お読みになっていらっしゃる, are fine. [source: https://www.bunka.go.jp/seisaku/bunkashingikai/sokai/sokai_6/pdf/keigo_tousin.pdf | confidence: high | 2007-02-02]
- **「(さ)せていただく」.** Use it only when you act with the listener's permission and benefit from it. 「発表いたします」 is simpler than 「発表させていただきます」. In a self-introduction, 「〇〇を卒業させていただきました」 is judged wrong. [source: https://www.bunka.go.jp/seisaku/bunkashingikai/sokai/sokai_6/pdf/keigo_tousin.pdf | confidence: high | 2007-02-02]
- **Words for "I" and "you".** In interviews say わたし or わたくし, not 僕. 「あなた」 is not suitable toward superiors. [source: https://www.bunka.go.jp/seisaku/bunkashingikai/sokai/sokai_6/pdf/keigo_tousin.pdf | confidence: high | 2007-02-02]
- **Common mistakes (Rikunabi).**
  - Say 御社 when speaking and 貴社 in writing.
  - Avoid おっしゃられる and ご覧になられる. Avoid 拝見させていただきます; say 拝見します.
  - Avoid 了解しました; say 承知しました or かしこまりました.
  - Avoid part-time-job keigo: よろしかったでしょうか, 〜になります, 履歴書の方. [source: https://job.rikunabi.com/contents/manners/32620/ | confidence: high | 2026-07-24]
- **Casual patterns to replace.** Don't start a sentence with 「なので」; use 「そのため」. Replace 「〜んじゃないですか」 with 「〜のではないでしょうか」. [source: https://www.9640.jp/BOOK_PDF/9784874249475.pdf | confidence: high]
- **In-person interview flow (JASSO).**
  - Before: arrive 15 min early. Switch your phone off and take your coat off before entering the building.
  - Reception: give your school, your name and why you are there. Wait quietly with your bag at your feet.
  - Entering: knock 2–3 times (Kurosio says 3). After 「どうぞ」, say 「失礼します」 and go in.
  - Greeting: bow about 30°, then give your school, faculty and name.
  - Sitting: sit after 「どうぞおかけください」, saying 「失礼します」. Sit upright without leaning back.
  - Leaving: stand and say 「本日はありがとうございました」, then bow again at the door. Put your coat on outside. [source: https://www.jasso.go.jp/ryugaku/after_study_j/job/__icsFiles/afieldfile/2026/10/30/guide_2028_39-77.pdf | confidence: high | 2026-10]
- **Web interview (JASSO).**
  - Log in 5 min early. Set the camera at eye level and look at it when you speak.
  - If you get disconnected, contact the company by chat or phone. Have a backup device and update the app beforehand.
  - At the end, thank them and bow; disconnect only after the interviewer does.
  - Interviewers notice when you read from notes.
  - Recorded video interviews: 1–2 min per question, and retakes are often allowed. [source: https://www.jasso.go.jp/ryugaku/after_study_j/job/__icsFiles/afieldfile/2026/10/30/guide_2028_39-77.pdf | confidence: high | 2026-10]
- **Extra Web checklist items.** Tidy background, bright lighting, a quiet room, screen and camera position checked, a stable connection, and a test of the tool beforehand. [source: https://www.pref.wakayama.lg.jp/prefg/060600/ui/ui_gaido/d00203618_d/fil/UI-013014.pdf | confidence: medium]

**Not found**
- Official interview guidance on ら抜き言葉 (見れる, 来れる). The safe choice is 見られる, 来られる.

## 6. Recommendations (synthesis of sections 1–5; no new facts)

**Daily core: about 75 min.** On busy days, do steps 1, 2 and 4 at 10 min each.
1. **Chunks, 10 min.** Spaced-repetition review, saying each full sentence aloud. Add at most 10 new interview chunks a day.
2. **Shadowing, 15 min.** Use one corrected answer script. Play the text-to-speech model at 0.8×, then 1.0×. Move up steps 1–5 during the week. Aim for at least 8 full passes per script per week, spread over 3 or more days.
3. **瞬間作文, 10 min.** 20 cue cards: an English or native-language cue, answered in Japanese within about 3 s. Build them on the answer pattern 結論 → 理由 → 具体例 → 結果 → 入社後の貢献.
4. **Timed answers, 15 min.** Explain the research as a solo 4/3/2, recording each round. Answer two other questions in 30 s and in 60 s.
5. **Record, transcribe, correct, 15 min.** One answer a day. Mark pauses, fillers and errors, then fix them. Make text-to-speech audio of the corrected script; it becomes tomorrow's shadowing script.
6. **OJAD, 5–10 min.** Check the accent of the katakana and technical words in today's answer with スズキクン. Drill ー, ッ and ン slowly, then at normal speed. Browser text-to-speech can put accents in the wrong place. Don't embed OJAD audio in the page.

**Weekly**
- 1–2 sessions with a person, 45–60 min each (tutor, career center, 留学生コーナー or センター). Ask them: 「間違いがあったら、すぐに正解を言わずに、まずヒントをください。」
- One AI voice mock interview of 20–30 min, starting in week 3.
- One 10-minute benchmark recording.

**Phases**
- **Weeks 1–2, scripted.** Write 8–10 core answers and get them corrected:
  - a 30-s self-introduction and a 1-min self-PR;
  - the research in 30 s, 1 min and 3 min;
  - ガクチカ and 志望動機;
  - strengths and weaknesses;
  - why Japan and how long you plan to work here;
  - an unwanted department;
  - three 逆質問.
- **Weeks 3–4, semi-scripted.** Keyword cards only; 4/3/2; one 深掘り follow-up per answer.
- **Weeks 5–6, unscripted.** Random and unseen questions. In the 年末年始 week, use online tutors and AI, and check each office's calendar.
- **Weeks 7–8, simulation.** Full in-person and Web run-throughs with people, then the final benchmark.

**Benchmark** (one practised prompt and one unseen prompt, 60–90 s each)
- Speed: characters per minute in the transcript. About 300 is the usual guideline.
- Mean length of run: characters between pauses of 0.3 s or more.
- Pauses in mid-clause per minute, and fillers (えー, あのー) per minute.
- Errors per 100 characters (grammar, particles, keigo), and self-corrections.
- Answer length against the target, and seconds before the first word.

**Minimal keigo set for an N3 speaker**
わたくし／〜と申します／〜しております／〜いたします／〜と考えております／拝見しました／伺います／おっしゃる／御社・貴社／承知いたしました／失礼いたします／恐れ入りますが、もう一度おっしゃっていただけますか／少し考える時間をいただいてもよろしいでしょうか／本日はよろしくお願いいたします／本日はありがとうございました

**AI mock-interview prompt** (paste as text, then switch to voice)
「あなたは〔企業名〕の〔職種〕の新卒採用面接官（一次面接）です。面接官の発言だけをしてください。質問は一度に一つずつ、私の回答ごとに一回深掘りしてください。5問終わったら、英語で①結論から話せているか②敬語の誤り③フィラーや長い間を指摘してください。誤りはまずヒントだけ示し、私が言い直してから正しい表現を示してください。」
Do not paste unpublished research data or personal details.
